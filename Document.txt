

Chapter 1: Introduction
The business-to-business (B2B) distribution industry increasingly relies on centralized, data-driven technologies to optimize supply chain and financial operations. However, many established enterprises, such as Active Pro Marketing Corporation, still rely heavily on disconnected manual processes that lead to operational bottlenecks and inaccurate financial reporting. This study proposes the development of an Integrated Inventory and Financial Reporting System grounded in the ITIL 4 Service Configuration Management framework to address these service gaps. This chapter presents the Company Background, Project Context, Purpose and Objectives, Scope and Limitations, and Project Milestones/Timeline.
Chapter 1
Company Background
Active Pro Marketing Corporation is a full-scale B2B distribution entity based in Valenzuela, specializing in supplying components and accessories to local bike shop owners and distributors. Operating from two large warehouses, the company manages an extensive inventory network to meet the demands of its growing client base. By providing critical supplies to local retailers, Active Pro plays a vital role in maintaining the supply chain and operational efficiency of the local cycling industry.
Project Context
The need for this project arises from the ongoing operational struggles within Active Pro Marketing Corporation's supply chain and financial tracking processes. Currently, the organization relies on manual documentation to manage stock lifecycles and agent orders to truck deliveries, while utilizing an unsynchronized manual encoding of Excel for accounting. On a daily basis, the company handles a high volume of inventory movements across its two facilities, which management attributes to the current reliance on manual systems that lack real-time synchronization. This manual-to-computerized conversion of data is prone to severe errors, leading to data silos, delayed fulfillment, inaccurate profit-and-loss (P&L) reporting, revenue leakage, and recurring non-payment tracking issues.
Purpose and Objectives
To address these operational challenges, this study proposes a web-based Integrated Inventory and Financial Reporting System. Utilizing the ITIL 4 Service Configuration Management framework, this project will centralize data across both warehouse facilities and automate the service lifecycle, from real-time B2B order monitoring to exact financial expense mapping. The following subsections detail the overarching purpose of the system development, as well as the specific objectives required to achieve it.
General Objectives
The general objective of this project is to design, develop, and implement an Integrated Inventory and Financial Reporting System for Active Pro Marketing Corporation in order to transition the company from manual counting and disconnected record-keeping to a centralized digital workflow that ensures data accuracy, operational reliability, and cost-efficient service fulfillment.

Specific Objectives
To achieve the general objective, this project specifically aims to:
Design a centralized multi-warehouse inventory control module with real-time, per-warehouse visual tracking, and a strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) architecture to secure sensitive business data and restrict unauthorized access to financial modules.


Develop a custom logistics optimization algorithm to efficiently manage delivery routes and reduce shipping costs independently of third-party AI, alongside an automated financial reporting module that explicitly categorizes profit and loss.
Evaluate the improvement in Service Level Agreement (SLA) compliance, fulfillment times, shipping cost reductions, and financial data accuracy using User Acceptance Testing (UAT) with Active Pro management and staff.
Scope and Limitations
The scope of this project includes the development of multi-warehouse inventory control, real-time B2B order monitoring, and automated internal financial reporting modules specifically designed for Service Provider users (office secretaries, managers) and Customers (field agents acting as internal clients). The system will generate specific outputs, including automated Profit-and-Loss (P&L) statements and SLA Compliance Reports. However, the system is limited strictly to internal supply chain and financial tracking, and explicitly excludes external taxation filing (BIR) and payroll processing functionalities. Furthermore, the project is constrained to a zero-budget requirement, utilizing free and open-source development tools such as MySQL. However, this constraint applies only to the development phase. The operational use of the system by the client may require subscription-based services necessary for deployment and hosting.

Review of Related Literature and Studies
Digital Transformation and IT Governance in MSMEs
Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) face distinct operational hurdles that threaten their viability in highly competitive global markets. They routinely operate with restricted capital, inadequate technical infrastructure, and limited human resources. To survive these constraints, organizations must modernize. Rather than merely adopting isolated technological tools, MSMEs must leverage digital transformation as a comprehensive strategy to overhaul internal workflows and expand their market reach (Triwahyono et al., 2023). When these technological innovations are properly integrated, businesses can significantly reduce operating costs while maintaining agility. However, achieving this level of modernization is rarely straightforward.
Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems represent a primary mechanism for facilitating this shift. While traditionally viewed as mere administrative software, ERP functions as a unifying organizational framework explicitly designed to dismantle internal data silos (Kaur, 2026). In many MSMEs, departments such as finance, human resources, and supply chain management operate in strict isolation from one another, breeding severe data inconsistencies and delayed reporting. Merging ERP infrastructure with Customer Relationship Management (CRM) systems addresses these exact friction points. Establishing a centralized database fosters cross-departmental collaboration and ensures the real-time supply chain visibility needed to track inventory and predict demand fluctuations (Zaman, 2024). These data silos are operationally dangerous for MSMEs, directly causing delayed order fulfillment, inaccurate reporting, and poor decision-making. Instead of operating from disjointed transactional data, managers gain the actionable insights needed to align their supply capabilities with actual customer expectations.
Despite these theoretical benefits, translating integrated platforms into actual operational success remains remarkably difficult for smaller enterprises. Pervasive digital illiteracy and rigid financial constraints routinely derail software deployments before they can yield any meaningful return on investment (Opoku et al., 2024). Levstek et al. (2022) suggest, the root of this failure often lies in governance rather than the technology itself. Standardized information technology governance frameworks were engineered specifically for the hierarchical complexities of large multinational corporations. When forced upon the flatter organizational structures of MSMEs, these universal compliance models collapse under their own administrative weight (Levstek et al., 2022). To avoid deployment failure, smaller firms require a highly adaptive, contingency-based governance approach. Software integration must be paired directly with situational oversight and sustained employee upskilling (Opoku et al., 2024). Without this adaptive governance, MSMEs inevitably revert to familiar manual practices. Disjointed legacy systems persist, perpetuating the very data silos and delayed order fulfillments the new technology was meant to eliminate. These operational breakdowns underscore the critical need for an ITIL-aligned service management approach that provides structured workflows while remaining lightweight enough for MSMEs to sustain.
ITIL 4 Implementation and Service Configuration Management
Effective information technology governance requires aligning strategic objectives with daily operational realities. Organizations often bridge this gap through the Information Technology Infrastructure Library (ITIL) framework. Rather than a rigid set of rules, ITIL serves as a customizable baseline of procedures and controls that dictate how IT services operate within complex enterprise environments (Santosa & Mulyana, 2023). This flexibility makes ITIL 4 especially relevant for businesses migrating from fragmented, manual workflows to integrated digital platforms. Without such structured governance, technological upgrades risk amplifying existing administrative inefficiencies rather than resolving them.
At the core of this framework is Service Configuration Management, a practice that ensures the accuracy and availability of service asset information. The mechanism driving this process is the Configuration Management Database (CMDB). A properly architected CMDB maps out all configuration items and their functional interdependencies, establishing a single, authoritative source of truth across the organization (Dande et al., 2024). This structural mapping directly attacks the chronic lack of operational visibility common in growing enterprises. When departments rely on isolated software tools, communication breaks down and data silos inevitably emerge. A centralized configuration repository dismantles these structural barriers, enabling synchronized, cross-departmental service delivery.
Despite these structural advantages, unified configuration tracking carries significant risks if deployed without adequate process design. CMDB initiatives frequently become unmanageable when organizations bypass structured process design or fail to align the database with core business objectives (Dande et al., 2024). Avoiding these implementation failures demands strict adherence to a standardized service data model before any software integration begins. For MSMEs, the stakes of this structural transition are high. Attempting to modernize without an ITIL-aligned service management strategy leaves these enterprises exposed to the persistent data silos and delayed fulfillment cycles they originally sought to eliminate.
Process Improvement through Automated Service Delivery
Systematic process improvement is a fundamental requirement for reliable service delivery. In retail and distribution, operational bottlenecks often manifest as persistent stock-outs, which degrade customer loyalty and erode profit margins. Small- and medium-scale operations frequently attempt to manage these risks using visual shelf inspections and physical ledgers. However, this reliance on manual tracking actively undermines inventory control rather than supporting it (Weerarathna, 2025). Organizations must structurally shift toward automated frameworks to align their replenishment cycles with actual demand. Modernizing these internal processes does more than replace paper ledgers; it systematically eliminates the daily operational friction that drains employee time and suppresses overall enterprise productivity (Kallmuenzer et al., 2024). Achieving this balance requires moving beyond reactive manual processes toward dynamic, system-driven monitoring.
Transitioning to an automated infrastructure accelerates the entire service delivery lifecycle by establishing a single source of operational truth. When organizations calculate material requirements in real time based on live sales metrics, they bypass the manual data entry errors that typically compromise traditional inventory processes (Sudarmi & Sunaryo, 2024). Beyond basic error reduction, embedding digital supply chains into daily workflows actively forces resource-constrained enterprises to continuously innovate their operational processes (Opoku et al., 2024). The contrast between legacy methods and integrated platforms is evident in documented cases. Firms that manage workflows through fragmented spreadsheets and messaging applications suffer from prolonged processing times and severe fulfillment discrepancies, whereas migrating to centralized automation resolves these exact vulnerabilities (Siregar et al., 2025). This structural weakness is especially critical for MSMEs operating within rapidly expanding B2B distribution sectors. As transaction volume scales, manual inventory management across multiple facilities inevitably fractures into isolated data silos. Without an ITIL-aligned service management framework to govern these processes, disconnected systems will continuously trigger stock-outs and delayed fulfillments, paralyzing an enterprise's ability to compete.
Service Level Agreement (SLA) Management and Monitoring
Service Level Agreements (SLAs) function as the primary accountability mechanism between service providers and clients. In modern supply chains, maintaining trust requires strict adherence to specific Service Level Objectives, such as maximum acceptable delivery times or system uptime thresholds (Khan et al., 2021). However, manually enforcing these metrics results in significant reporting inaccuracies. When human operators must track geographically dispersed deliveries, administrative bottlenecks make it unclear whether providers actually meet their contractual obligations. Manual oversight cannot scale. To prevent these localized failures from eroding customer trust, Khan et al. (2021) argue that organizations must adopt automated, transparent verification systems. Such system-driven audits shift performance monitoring from a reactive administrative chore to an objective structural guarantee.
Transitioning to real-time compliance assessment directly addresses these oversight limitations. Automated frameworks autonomously extract operational metrics, such as processing duration, and evaluate them against stipulated quality parameters without human intervention (Alzubaidi et al., 2023). This delegation eliminates subjective reporting biases and provides immediate visibility into SLA breaches. However, monitoring alone is insufficient if the performance data remains isolated from the rest of the business. Engel et al. (2022) advocate for SLA-aware operational efficiency, a paradigm that embeds granular SLA analytics directly into core business workflows. Treating compliance as an active component of daily operations allows enterprises to balance engagement-specific service commitments with broader process optimization. For resource-constrained MSMEs, this integration is vital. Without a unified, ITIL-aligned approach to service management, disjointed tracking methods inevitably produce data silos, masking the root causes of delayed fulfillments and leaving service quality vulnerable to systemic blind spots.
Customer Service Digitization and Satisfaction
Digital transformation must extend beyond internal inventory control to redefine how organizations interact with their clients. Online transaction mechanisms do not merely facilitate sales; they actively reshape customer expectations by demanding immediate, personalized responsiveness. Mahmoud et al. (2024) argue that integrating these digital touchpoints enables enterprises to bypass geographical limitations and deploy real-time predictive analytics. Anticipating client needs through operational data has become essential for sustaining long-term customer retention. Leveraging backend data to streamline frontend interactions serves as the primary mechanism for mitigating customer churn and securing long-term loyalty (Brunner et al., 2025).
The technical viability of this customer-centric approach depends entirely on Application Programming Interfaces (APIs). Bridging legacy inventory structures with modern digital storefronts closes a critical operational gap. APIs enable the real-time transmission of product availability, order status, and transaction records across systems (Dubey et al., 2024). Seamless synchronization empowers businesses to provide proactive support and accurate delivery estimates. This continuous flow of data allows organizations to absorb client feedback and immediately optimize supply chain workflows (Brunner et al., 2025). When these backend-to-frontend integration points are absent, however, customer-facing systems become detached from the operational realities driving them. For MSMEs, this disconnect signals a deeper governance failure: without ITIL-aligned service management anchoring the digital infrastructure, backend data silos surface as unreliable order tracking and delayed fulfillment on the client-facing side, directly undermining the customer satisfaction these systems were designed to protect.
Barriers to Technological Adoption and Legacy System Migration
While integrated platforms offer clear operational benefits, MSMEs rarely adopt them seamlessly. High capital requirements and a severe digital skills gap routinely stall modernization efforts. However, financial constraints alone do not explain the problem. Organizational inertia plays an equally powerful role. Managers frequently exhibit technological path dependency, clinging to obsolete legacy systems solely because they remain nominally functional (Kallmuenzer et al., 2024). This persistent reliance on outdated infrastructure actively generates interoperability conflicts when firms attempt to integrate modern software with entrenched IT frameworks (Koushik, 2025). Risk-averse organizational cultures further complicate the transition, as employees who have spent years performing manual bookkeeping often reject new interfaces, generating widespread resistance that magnifies the inherent uncertainty of digital transformation (Kallmuenzer et al., 2024).
Navigating these deep-rooted technical and cultural barriers requires a highly strategic approach to implementation. Attempting a complete system overhaul often backfires. Instead, deploying core modules such as basic inventory management first allows staff to adapt to digital workflows before interacting with more sophisticated features (Koushik, 2025). Cloud-based subscription models provide a valuable workaround, offering scalable access to advanced tools without the prohibitive upfront costs of replacing physical hardware. Selecting the appropriate legacy systems for migration also benefits from structured decision-making tools such as the Technology Acceptance Model, which focuses on Perceived Usefulness and Perceived Ease of Use to help managers predict adoption success and align system capabilities with user expectations (Salih, 2025). However, simply acquiring flexible cloud tools or rolling out software in phases is insufficient if the underlying governance remains fragmented. When piecemeal migrations occur without robust, ITIL-aligned service management, MSMEs inadvertently create new operational bottlenecks. Departments end up mastering disconnected modules, trapping critical information in data silos that ultimately cause the exact delayed fulfillment issues the digital transition was meant to resolve.
Review of Related Systems and Technologies
Limitations of Generic Business and Inventory Systems
While generic business applications and accounting software provide foundational transaction recording, they inherently lack the infrastructure required for IT Service Management (ITSM). Small-scale point-of-sale (POS) and inventory tools such as Peddlr, Kyte, Loyverse, and Aronium are designed primarily for financial tracking and basic quantitative inventory counts rather than the comprehensive lifecycle management of IT services. Similarly, mid-tier solutions like SalesBinder, inFlow Inventory, and ZarMoney focus heavily on managing customer bookings and stock levels but fail to provide a Configuration Management Database (CMDB) or automated mechanisms for Service Level Agreement (SLA) breach escalation. Even comprehensive Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) platforms, such as Odoo, NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics 365, ERPNext, and Zoho One. While robust in supply chain and financial operations, they do not inherently offer CI-based asset management tailored for IT governance. The absence of such ITSM-specific features across these applications means organizations cannot effectively map dependencies between configuration items or automate incident responses. Consequently, relying on these generic platforms leads to manual operational bottlenecks, data silos, and a strictly reactive approach to problem management.
IT Governance and Service Management Frameworks
Establishing structured IT governance is imperative for mitigating operational risks and ensuring service continuity. Evaluating current governance capabilities often relies on frameworks like COBIT 2019, which provides a comprehensive baseline for assessing process maturity in areas such as incident and problem management (Idrus & Widodo, 2025). However, comprehensive standards are frequently unsuitable for Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs) due to their high implementation costs, limited employee resources, and structural heterogeneity (Deistler & Rentrop, 2022). To bridge this gap, organizations must adapt appropriately scaled frameworks. The integration of ITIL 4 and FitSM offers a balanced approach for resource-constrained environments (Idrus & Widodo, 2025). Specifically, adopting ITIL 4 Service Configuration Management establishes a structured method for tracking every Configuration Item (CI), ensuring that all hardware, software, and service dependencies are accurately recorded. While FitSM provides the lightweight processes necessary for daily operations, ITIL 4 supplies the definitive structural depth required to govern CIs systematically
Digital Transformation and Information System Acceptance
The transition toward structured ITSM infrastructure requires more than technical deployment; it necessitates a fundamental cultural shift and strong user acceptance to succeed. Implementing enterprise platforms such as ServiceNow, which centralize data and standardize workflows, significantly improves operational efficiency and reduces incident resolution times (Bhat & Dhiman, 2024). Nevertheless, the successful adoption of such new information systems is heavily dependent on user perception and proactive early-stage communication (Fabbian, 2023). The initial phases of digital transformation are critical because convincing internal stakeholders of long-term benefits often presents a major obstacle to process optimization. By prioritizing transparent communication and establishing trust, organizations can foster an innovation-oriented mindset that mitigates resistance to change (Fabbian, 2023). These insights directly inform the design of User Acceptance Testing (UAT) and preliminary workshop phases, ensuring that impacted users develop the necessary competence and confidence to operate integrated ITSM workflows effectively prior to full deployment.
Automation in Incident Management and Service Delivery
The integration of automated tools into incident management value chains drastically reduces manual workloads and accelerates service delivery. Utilizing automation features within systems like Jira enables support teams to execute tasks more efficiently by generating automatic alerts for critical service requests and streamlining backlog monitoring (Tistelgrén, 2023). Advancements in artificial intelligence, as explored by Samala (2025), further enhance these capabilities by enabling machine learning algorithms to optimize resource allocation and automate ticket routing without human intervention. These predictive analytics and automated SLA escalation mechanisms minimize operational errors and drastically shorten incident resolution times. Despite the substantial operational effectiveness delivered by enterprise-grade platforms such as Jira and ServiceNow, these systems remain highly complex and cost-prohibitive for Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs). The extensive configuration requirements and recurring subscription fees prevent smaller operations from leveraging advanced queue automation, highlighting the necessity for scalable, purpose-built alternatives.
Synthesis
The reviewed systems reveal a consistent trade-off between governance depth and affordability, leaving MSMEs chronically underserved. Mobile and standalone tools such as Peddlr, Kyte, and Loyverse offer accessible stock tracking. However, they are built exclusively for business retail, with no CMDB functionality, SLA monitoring, or escalation workflows. Mid-tier platforms such as SalesBinder, inFlow, and ZarMoney address isolated operational segments, stock partitioning, barcode fulfillment, and financial reporting, but remain transactional, tracking what was sold rather than governing the service lifecycle of the configuration items behind each transaction. Enterprise platforms such as Odoo, NetSuite, and Zoho One achieve the necessary operational breadth but impose prohibitive licensing costs and IT administration requirements, placing them beyond the reach of MSMEs. IT Service Management platforms such as ServiceNow and Jira come closest to ITIL 4 alignment through their SLA tracking and CI management capabilities, yet their architecture targets internal IT departments managing software incidents, not B2B supply chains where a configuration item is a physical warehouse asset and a service request is a bulk field order. No reviewed system integrates ITIL 4 Service Configuration Management principles within an architecture that is simultaneously affordable and operationally suited to MSME B2B distribution. This gap is precisely what the proposed Integrated Inventory and Financial Reporting System is designed to fill.

Business Process
Active Pro Marketing Corporation operates as a nationwide B2B distributor of bicycle parts, accessories, and complete bikes. The existing business process is characterized by an outdated legacy software system that significantly degrades service delivery. Currently, the lifecycle of physical inventory, acting as critical Configuration Items (CIs), is disjointed. Furthermore, the current system lacks multimedia capabilities; it cannot store images of the physical assets, severely crippling the staff's ability to maintain a definitive visual digital reference for the inventory. This lack of visual Configuration Item (CI) mapping increases the risk of asset misidentification and warehouse picking errors.
In contrast, the proposed business process introduces a centralized digital architecture that automates Configuration Item (CI) tracking and incorporates digital image repositories. This technological intervention completely bypasses physical verification barriers, representing a critical shift from reactive incident management to controlled service fulfillment. Integrating an enterprise architecture designed around ITIL 4 best practices provides a direct blueprint for transforming these delayed operational states into highly governed digital service workflows (Santosa & Mulyana, 2023).

Business Requirements Overview
The business requirements for the Integrated Inventory and Financial Reporting System (IIFRS) were derived directly from the semi-structured interviews with the Chief Marketing Officer (CMO) and the rigorous process analysis of Active Pro Marketing Corporation's current manual operations. These specifications define the mandatory systemic capabilities and operational constraints required to successfully implement automated Service Configuration Management across the organization's supply chain. Furthermore, to ensure long-term business alignment, the system's architecture is designed to be highly scalable; the database logic permits the seamless addition of a third or fourth warehouse node should the company expand its physical distribution network in the future. The following subsections explicitly outline the automated functionalities, interface mock-ups, and system-generated reports necessary to resolve the company's existing data silos and SLA fulfillment delays.
Functional Requirements
The system must execute critical functions to sustain the proposed automated workflow and ensure strict adherence to ITIL 4 Service Configuration Management. Specifically, the system must autonomously update the status of all relevant Configuration Items (CIs) within the centralized Configuration Management Database (CMDB) in real time upon transaction logging. Furthermore, the SLA Monitoring engine must actively track fulfillment timestamps against predefined geographical baselines, triggering automated escalation protocols to specific Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) groups upon detecting a breach. The explicit functional requirements engineered for the Integrated Inventory and Financial Reporting System (IIFRS) are detailed in Table 3.3.1 below.

FR-01 
Mutli-Warehouse Visual Tracking & Synchronization
The system shall display real-time stock levels partitioned visually per individual warehouse facility and record real-time inventory deductions synchronously upon item transfers or B2B transaction logs.
FR-02
Digital CI Image Repository
The system must provide a dedicated digital repository to store and link photo reference to specific CI profiles, enabling staff to instantly retrieve product images for Facebook Messenger inquiries.
FR-04
Dynamic Price List Generation
The system shall automatically extract active CI data from the CMDB to generate exportable monthly price lists, dynamically sorting inventories by brand, size, and new arrivals without requiring manual data entry.

Categorized Financial Tracking
The system must autonomously map operational transactions to the financial ledger in real time, explicitly separating and filtering the dashboard display into distinct profit and loss categories.

SLA Monitoring and Automated Escalation
The system shall continuously track fulfillment timestamps against the 1-week (Metro Manila) and 2-week (Mindanao) SLAs. Upon detecting a timeline breach, the system must automatically generate an ITIL incident ticket and trigger an in-system escalation alert containing the Order ID, the delayed CI details, and the customer location.
FR-07
Restrictive Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
The system must enforce strict RBAC protocols, granting full administrative control to the CMO, while explicitly restricting Office Staff (Secretaries) from editing financial records and completely revoking Field Agents' access to financial modules and automated reporting.

The technological feasibility of this project is deemed high. The client organization currently possesses the necessary personal computers on-site to act as localized administrative terminals. Assessing organizational feasibility reveals a moderate need for change management. Additionally, a comprehensive user training phase will be executed for all end-users from the office secretaries down to the field agents to ensure a smooth transition from manual to automated workflows.

An economic feasibility analysis was conducted, demonstrating high viability for the organization. Development Methodology

This project employs a developmental research design driven by a tailored Agile Scrum Methodology to systematically address Active Pro Marketing Corporation’s specific operational requirements. To ensure the system accurately resolved the company's bottlenecks, the problem was studied through the framework of ITIL 4 Service Configuration Management principles, evaluating how physical inventory transitions into a governed digital lifecycle. Guided by these principles, the development lifecycle was explicitly structured into targeted, iterative sprints based on the system's core functional modules, initially focusing on engineering the foundational Configuration Management Database (CMDB) schema and a digital visual repository before advancing to automated order routing and intelligent financial tracking algorithms. Throughout this process, primary data and system requirements were gathered through direct, in-depth interviews with the Chief Marketing Officer (CMO), who acted as the definitive Product Owner. By integrating stakeholder interviews into the Agile process through active participation in sprint reviews, the team continuously gathered both qualitative insights and quantitative data from the company’s internal financial ledger and operational records. This ensured that the financial modules and automated SLA tracking were consistently validated against real-world conditions.
Software Quality Assurance Plans
To guarantee that the Integrated Inventory and Financial Reporting System meets the specific operational needs of Active Pro Marketing Corporation, a comprehensive Quality Assurance (QA) plan will be executed prior to full-scale deployment. The primary objective of this phase is to systematically validate the system's data accuracy, operational stability, and overall usability as the organization transitions from disconnected, manual paper ledgers to a centralized digital workflow. Guided by standard software quality models, the evaluation will rigorously verify that the automated multi-warehouse inventory deductions and the Profit and Loss (P&L) calculations function flawlessly. Furthermore, the QA plan is specifically designed to test the system's reliability under the client's budgetary constraints ensuring the lightweight cloud server can consistently handle the mandatory photo-upload validations and the automated Service Level Agreement (SLA) escalation alerts without performance degradation. To achieve these quality benchmarks, the system will undergo three critical evaluation phases: Unit Testing, Stress Testing, and User Acceptance Testing (UAT).
Unit Test
This section involves several unit tests that will test the functionality of each requirement and ensure that it will meet the requirements needed. Each aspect of the system, from basic functionality to complex functionality, undergoes testing to verify its functionality. Through this test, any discrepancies can be identified and can be improved to provide a reliable experience.
Unit Test Criteria
To successfully pass this testing phase, the established criteria require a 100% pass rate on all core data validation checks. Because the system will handle sensitive inventory data across two locations and track financial metrics, there is zero tolerance for miscalculations in the multi-warehouse stock deductions or the Profit and Loss (P&L) tracking. Each isolated function must execute exactly as intended without triggering backend database errors; any component that fails to meet this strict binary standard will be immediately flagged and returned to the development team for recoding.
Unit Test Scenario
A primary test scenario will involve the tester inputting a simulated bulk business-to-business (B2B) order into the order processing module. The expected outcome is that the system accurately calculates the total, deducts the specific bike part quantities from the correct warehouse database, and automatically triggers the "queue cut" alert for the downstairs preparation staff. Furthermore, the tester will attempt to update the simulated order status to "Out for Delivery" without attaching an image; the expected outcome is that the system successfully blocks the transaction and displays an error mandating a photo upload.
Stress Test
Given the organization's approved budget constraint of utilizing a lightweight $7-$15 monthly cloud server, stress testing is critical. The system will be subjected to simulated high-volume scenarios to test server stability and storage limitations. This includes executing concurrent data entries (simulating multiple field agents querying stock simultaneously) and performing bulk uploads of high-resolution photographic evidence to ensure the server does not crash or exhaust its storage capacity during peak operational hours.
Stress Test Criteria 
To pass this phase, the cloud-based system must successfully process concurrent heavy-data requests without experiencing server crashes, database deadlocks, or HTTP 500/504 timeout errors. Specifically, the system must maintain a page load and query response time of under three seconds even when the server's bandwidth and storage I/O are stretched. Furthermore, the database must not exhaust its allocated cloud memory limit when handling simultaneous bulk data entries or when compressing incoming media files.
Stress Test Scenario 
The primary stress test scenario will simulate peak operational hours by generating concurrent system traffic. Using an automated load-testing tool, the development team will simulate multiple remote field agents continuously querying the multi-warehouse inventory from mobile connections. Simultaneously, testers will simulate the downstairs warehouse staff uploading multiple high-resolution photographic files (simulating the required proof of prepared B2B orders) directly into the dispatch module. The objective is to verify that the lightweight server can securely save the required photo validations without locking out the field agents trying to view real-time stock levels.
User-Acceptance Test
User Acceptance Testing will be the final evaluation phase, involving the actual end-users: the CMO, office secretaries, and agents. Utilizing anonymized sample forms and legacy processes provided by management, the staff will perform a live simulation of the new workflow from B2B order placement to photo attachment and final SLA tracking. Because the staff has varying levels of technological proficiency (with older staff preferring manual writing), the UAT will heavily evaluate the UI's user-friendliness and accessibility. The CMO will sign the final User Acceptance Form following a successful online defense and demonstration of the system's accuracy.
User-Acceptance Test Criteria
The primary passing criterion for UAT relies on the successful adoption of the system by the older office personnel who are heavily accustomed to legacy processes. The system will pass if these staff members can navigate the digital forms and process an order without requiring constant IT intervention, validating that the UI successfully mimics their traditional physical logbooks. Additionally, for the financial and administrative modules, the system must receive formal sign-off from the Chief Marketing Officer (CMO), certifying that the automated Profit and Loss (P&L) statements accurately reflect the raw data without errors.
User-Acceptance Test Scenario 
The UAT scenario will be a fully observed, live simulation utilizing anonymized data from the company's legacy manual logbooks. A staff will use the on-site shop PC to input a simulated B2B order, triggering the digital queue cut. The downstairs warehouse staff will then be tasked with using the system to upload a physical photo of the prepared items to authorize the dispatch. Finally, the CMO will access the overarching dashboard to verify if the transaction successfully updated the multi-warehouse inventory counts in real time and properly logged the pending payment in the financial tracker. Evaluators will actively monitor the staff's comfort level and note any navigational friction during the workflow.
Deployment Plans
The deployment of the Integrated Inventory and Financial Reporting System will utilize a Parallel Adoption strategy. For a period of two weeks, the organization will run the new cloud-based system alongside their traditional manual logbooks. This approach ensures business continuity; if the cloud server experiences unexpected downtime, the manual ledgers serve as an immediate backup.
The physical deployment will involve configuring the existing shop PCs as the primary administrative terminals for the secretaries, while the database itself is hosted on the approved lightweight cloud server for remote mobile access by the field agents. Furthermore, the deployment phase will feature a mandatory capacity-building and training week. Because older staff members are highly accustomed to pen-and-paper processes, targeted, hands-on training sessions will be conducted to guide them through navigating the digital forms, uploading photos, and processing the automated queues, ensuring a smooth cultural transition within the workplace.
